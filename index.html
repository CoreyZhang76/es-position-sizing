What works

  • The forecaster. Predicts whether the next few seconds are calm or busy, at an accuracy (AUC) of 0.83 to 0.90 on real ES.
  • Execution timing. Trading in the calm windows cuts the volatility traded through by about 29%. Confirmed on the real order book, and a net saving for orders up to about 0.5% of daily volume.
  • VIX term structure. The volatility risk premium is real and large, with VIX futures running 6.2 points above later realized volatility on 91% of days. A front against next calendar spread harvests it at about an eighth of the crash risk of a naked short, a crash beta of +0.48 against +3.68 over 2013 to 2026. It does not earn more per unit of risk than that naked short: 0.51 ±0.58 against 0.50 ±0.57, paired bootstrap p = 0.94. The win is structural rather than a free Sharpe, because it trades the shape and sheds the crash.
  • Micro to macro. The two scales connect. The intraday forecaster's calm or busy call works as a risk filter for the term-structure trade, flagging when to step out of short volatility and cutting the naked short's drawdown from −89% to −62%. It flags the risky regime without predicting the curve's direction.

What did not work

  • Direction. Predictable (88% of the time) but smaller than the spread, so about zero after costs.
  • Position sizing. Controls risk and drawdown; it does not add return.
  • Cross-asset VIX. Adds nothing to the short-term ES forecast.

Status

  • Deployable now. The forecaster and a calm-window execution scheduler on ES.
  • Needs more. Desk execution data to price the timing saving in dollars, and more data months to test across regimes.
