Forecasting and trading volatility

What works

  • The forecaster. Calls whether the next ten seconds will be calm or busy, scoring 0.83 to 0.90 out of sample on ES.
  • Execution timing. Trading in the calm windows cuts the volatility an order passes through by about 29%, and remains a net saving for orders up to roughly 0.5% of daily volume.
  • VIX term structure. VIX futures price about 6 points above the volatility that follows, on 91% of days. Selling the front contract collects that premium and carries the full equity crash. Holding a later month against it collects a similar premium at roughly an eighth of the crash exposure, without earning more per unit of risk.
  • The two scales connect. The intraday calm or busy call works as a risk filter on the slower VIX trade. Stepping out of short volatility when it flags a busy regime cuts the outright short's worst drawdown from −89% to −62%, by identifying the regime rather than the direction.

What did not work

  • Predicting direction. Correct 88% of the time, but the move is smaller than the bid-ask spread, so nothing survives costs.
  • Position sizing as a source of return. It controls risk and drawdown without increasing return.
  • Using VIX to forecast ES. No incremental value over the ES order book.

Status

  • Ready to use. The forecaster, and a scheduler routing orders into calm windows on ES.
  • Still needed. Desk execution data to price the timing saving, and further months to test across regimes.
